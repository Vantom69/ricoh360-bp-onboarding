# EN tone naturalization grid — V2 Onboarding

Reference grid for translating the V2 site from FR to EN. Mirrors the validated French grille `grille-naturalisation-ton-fr.md` adapted to a B2B EN context.

The goal is **idiomatic, natural EN** that sounds written by a native — not translated word-for-word from French. Same Customer Success spirit, same level of warmth, but with EN syntax and rhythm.

---

## 1. Guiding principles

1. **Talk to a real beginner**, not write a technical manual.
2. **Prefer action**: imperative verbs, present simple, active voice.
3. **Short sentences**: one subject = one action = one outcome.
4. **Direct address to the reader**: "you", liberally but not excessively.
5. **Concision without dryness**: cut the filler, keep the supportive tone.
6. **Everyday vocabulary**, except for the agreed product terms (cf. §4).

---

## 2. What we avoid (over-translated / corporate flavors)

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Heavy nominalizations ("the activation of...", "the installation of...") | Sounds like a manual. Use a verb in the imperative. |
| French calques ("in order to", "it is important to", "permit you to") | EN has its own more direct turns. |
| Weak admin verbs ("proceed to", "perform", "carry out") | Use the direct action verb. |
| Excessive impersonal turns ("it is necessary to", "one must") | Use the second person or imperative. |
| Wordy connectors ("furthermore", "moreover", "henceforth") | Use simpler ones or none. |
| Empty reassuring doublets ("simple and easy", "clear and straightforward") | Pick one well-placed word. |
| French syntax leaked into EN (post-position adverbs, long subordinate clauses) | Restructure for EN cadence. |

---

## 3. What we prefer

| Prefer | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Imperative verbs at the start | Puts the reader in motion. |
| Direct address with "you" | Reader involvement. |
| Average 12-18 word sentences | Readability and rhythm. |
| Short connectors ("then", "next", "if", "when", "so") | Flow. |
| Concrete verbs ("tap", "click", "plug in") | Field-ready action. |
| Reassuring callouts for edge cases | Avoids overloading the main sentence. |
| One idea per sentence | Easier to scan. |

## 4. Product vocabulary to keep intact

These terms are **never translated** — they're brand or product identifiers:

- **RICOH360** — always uppercase.
- **RICOH THETA X**, **RICOH THETA A1** — exact casing.
- **RICOH360 Cloud** — product name.
- **RICOH360 App**, **RICOH360 WebApp**, **mobile app**, **web platform** — naming consistency.
- **Business Pack** — offer name (capitalized).
- **panorama 360°** — central business term (use `panorama` in EN, not `panoramic image`).
- **RICOH Care** — coverage product (note: brand uses RICOH Care, not Ricoh Care).
- **Register**, **Done** — UI button labels in the app, keep in EN.

General "tech-y" vocabulary to use thoughtfully:

- "Workflow" → fine in EN.
- "Set up" / "Setup" → prefer for hardware/account prep ("set up your camera" not "install your camera").
- "Sign in" → prefer over "log in" or "connect to your account" (Microsoft/Google style).
- "Tap" → for mobile UI (instead of "press").
- "Click" → for desktop UI.

## 6. Before / after examples

### Example 1 — Welcome intro

**Before** (FR):
> *Vous venez de recevoir votre Business Pack RICOH360 — bienvenue ! Cet espace vous guide de la réception de votre pack jusqu'aux usages avancés. Avancez à votre rythme : vous pouvez revenir piocher dedans à tout moment.*

**After** (EN):
> *You just received your RICOH360 Business Pack — welcome! This space guides you from unboxing all the way to advanced use cases. Go at your own pace and come back to any step whenever you need it.*

→ "Vous venez de recevoir" → "You just received". "Piocher dedans" → "come back to any step" (natural EN equivalent, not literal).

### Example 2 — Section 5 (connect the camera)

**Before** (FR):
> *Allumez la caméra et le smartphone, et vérifiez qu'ils sont bien chargés.*

**After** (EN):
> *Turn on the camera and your smartphone, and make sure they're well charged.*

### Example 3 — Section 8 (share)

**Before** (FR):
> *Choisissez les options de partage : durée du lien, mot de passe, droits d'accès. Les options disponibles dépendent de votre offre.*

**After** (EN):
> *Choose the sharing options: link duration, password, access rights. The available options depend on your plan.*

### Example 4 — Section 7 (Cloud)

**Before** (FR):
> *Tous vos panoramas suivent le même chemin. Votre caméra les capture, l'application mobile les transfère, le Cloud les synchronise automatiquement, et vous les retrouvez sur la plateforme web depuis n'importe quel ordinateur ou tablette.*

**After** (EN):
> *All your panoramas follow the same path. Your camera captures them, the mobile app transfers them, the Cloud syncs them automatically, and you find them on the web platform from any computer or tablet.*

### Example 5 — Section 11A (Construction)

**Before** (FR):
> *Dès le démarrage du chantier, repérez les points de capture clés — entrées, étages, zones critiques. Repassez par les mêmes points à chaque visite.*

**After** (EN):
> *Right from the start of the project, identify the key capture points — entrances, floors, critical areas. Revisit the same points on every site visit.*

### Example 6 — FAQ

**Before** (FR):
> *Le nombre de liens de partage actifs en même temps, ainsi que les options disponibles (durée, mot de passe, droits), dépendent de votre offre.*

**After** (EN):
> *The number of share links you can have active at the same time, and the options available (duration, password, rights), depend on your plan.*

### Example 7 — FAQ Ricoh Care

**Before** (FR):
> *Oui. Ricoh Care est inclus dans votre RICOH360 Business Pack. Ce service vous donne accès à un support dédié, aux mises à jour firmware et à une couverture de remplacement de la caméra selon les conditions prévues.*

**After** (EN):
> *Yes. RICOH Care is included in your RICOH360 Business Pack. The service gives you dedicated support, access to firmware updates, and camera replacement coverage as defined in the program terms.*

### Example 8 — Section 10A (annotations)

**Before** (FR):
> *Mettez-vous d'accord sur un vocabulaire commun avec votre équipe avant de démarrer. Vous trierez et filtrerez vos annotations bien plus facilement par la suite.*

**After** (EN):
> *Agree on a shared vocabulary with your team before you get started. You'll sort and filter your annotations much more easily later on.*

## 7. Pitfalls to watch

- **JourneyStep outcomes** ("Vous savez X" / "By the end of this step you'll know how to X") — keep the future-leaning, capability-focused wording.
- **CTAs** ("Démarrer" → "Get started", "Prochaine étape" → "Next step") — short and unambiguous.
- **FAQ questions** — sound like a real client question, not a manual rubric. "How do I check..." rather than "On how to check..."
- **Tip boxes** — conversational tone allowed. The most natural place to be close to the reader.
- **Tables** — keep parallel structure in EN (same grammatical form across all rows).
- **Numbered steps** — start with imperative verbs in EN, just like in FR.

---

## 8. Assumed limits

This grid does not require translating slugs (URLs stay identical : `/en/02-installer-camera/`, not `/en/02-set-up-camera/`). The following items remain frozen:

- Step names and section numbering.
- `<JourneyStep>` outcomes and objectives (except minor reformulation).
- Procedural facts (steps 1-2-3, values, durations).
- Product vocabulary (cf. §4).

The objective: **every sentence should sound natively written in EN**, without changing what the sentence says.
