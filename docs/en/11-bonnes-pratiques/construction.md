# 11A. Construction and site monitoring

<JourneyStep
  number="11A"
  objective="Adopt RICOH360 best practices to document a construction project and track its progress over time."
  outcome="You can structure your project documentation so it stays useful from one site visit to the next."
>

## The 5 essential best practices

### 1. Capture the same areas on every visit

Right from the start of the project, identify the **recurring capture points** — entrances, floors, critical zones. Walk through every one of those points on each site visit. That's the foundation of any time-based tracking.

### 2. Use floor plans to position your panoramas

Import the project's floor plan and **position each panorama** on it from the first visit. You'll then have a complete spatial overview and find the right capture spots in seconds.

[See how to position panoramas on a plan →](/en/09-organiser/plans)

### 3. Compare views from one visit to the next

After a few visits, the **Comparison feature** reveals the real progress of the project: newly installed elements, modified areas, points that haven't moved.

[See how to compare two panoramas →](/en/10-exploiter/comparer)

### 4. Annotate checkpoints directly on the panoramas

On each visit, **annotate** the points to revisit, the non-conformities you spot, or the interventions that were completed. Your annotations build a visual logbook the whole team can use.

[See how to add annotations →](/en/10-exploiter/annotations)

### 5. Organize by project / building / floor / zone

Adopt a consistent organization logic:

- **One project per construction project**
- **One album per building or phase**
- **A naming convention by floor or zone** in the panorama names

That's what lets you scale as the panorama volume grows.

::: tip Team convention
Agree on the organization structure and the annotation vocabulary with your team **before** you start a new project. It's much easier to do upfront than to fix at the end of the project.
:::
