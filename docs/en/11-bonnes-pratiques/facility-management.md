# 11C. Facility management and maintenance

<JourneyStep
  number="11C"
  objective="Adopt RICOH360 best practices to manage a property portfolio and document your maintenance interventions."
  outcome="You can structure your FM documentation so it stays useful to the field teams and to decision-makers."

## The 5 essential best practices

### 1. Document the technical areas and key equipment

List the **technical areas to document** (technical rooms, boiler rooms, elevators, server rooms) and the **critical equipment** that should appear in your content base.

### 2. Capture before and after each intervention

For each maintenance intervention:

- **Capture a panorama before** the intervention (starting state).
- **Capture a panorama after** the intervention (result).
- **Compare** the two to document the work done.

[See how to compare two panoramas →](/en/10-exploiter/comparer)

### 3. Organize by building / floor / zone / equipment

A typical organization structure in FM:

- **One project per building** or per site
- **One album per floor** or major technical area
- **A naming convention by zone or equipment** in the panoramas

This is what lets the whole FM team **quickly find** a visual of a specific piece of equipment.

### 4. Annotate actions and states

On each panorama, annotate:

- the **interventions completed** ("Filter changed on March 15")
- the **points to monitor** on the next visits
- the **anomalies you spot** that need to be handled

Your annotations become a **visual maintenance logbook** the whole team can consult.

[See how to add annotations →](/en/10-exploiter/annotations)

### 5. Keep a usable visual history

Preserve panoramas over time — they form a **visual memory** of the property:

- For future interventions
- For technical and insurance audits
- For team or contractor handovers

::: tip
Capture critical areas at regular intervals (quarterly, semi-annually) even when there's no intervention. You'll always have a fresh comparison base to spot any degradation.
:::
